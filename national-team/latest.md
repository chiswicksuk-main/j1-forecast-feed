## 4. Tactical observations worth writing about

The interesting question is not "back three or back four" as a piece of trivia. It is what Japan think went wrong after they won the ball in Houston. Moriyasu, on 4 Nov, asked for more attacking and defensive presses, a higher line, and more attempts to win the ball, and pointed to Japan being pushed back once Brazil's power increased in that last-16 defeat. Sano, on 10 Nov, said almost the same thing in plainer language: go from the front, and keep the ball for longer once you have it. That is the test, whatever the graphic says.

The Asian Cup delay has quietly changed the meaning of the camp. These were supposed to be the last A-team games before a tournament that has now moved. Doan told Sanspo this is likely the last full Europe-based trip under Moriyasu, and that the manager's motto, winning every game, has not changed. Sano told Soccer Digest the postponement is outside the players' control and that a national-team match still has the same weight. The football argument, then, is whether Japan use Brazil to prove they can get out twice in a row after a turnover, rather than treating the week as a farewell exhibition.

Ueda's absence is the actual selection problem, and the two predicted XIs are two different answers to the Houston issue. Sporting News starts Ogawa because Japan may spend time pinned in, and need someone who can hold the ball and bring Kubo and the wide players in. The live preview, and Kawaji's older 20 Oct note, prefer Maeda, a runner who can pull a centre-back and press. Ogawa and Maeda are not interchangeable in that argument. Writing "one of the strikers will start" would flatten it.

Minamino's return is being talked about as a limited role, which is exactly how Moriyasu framed it on 4 Nov. All 28 being on the pitch on 11 Nov is not the same as Minamino, Machida, Kubo or Fukuda being ready to start. Training is not clearance, and the useful session on Wednesday was closed.

On Brazil's side, the midfield is the open door. They are already a man light after Danilo was not replaced, and Cahê saw five names tried for three places on Wednesday. If Bruno is spared again, that uncertainty becomes the story. The other observation worth using is Raphinha: UOL says he will captain the side, and Cahê saw him trained as a centre-forward, with Estêvão and Vinícius either side. That is a different Brazil to the 10 Nov preview, which had Endrick through the middle and Raphinha still building minutes. Keep both, dated.

Ancelotti, per UOL, is likelier to give the experienced players the Japan game and save the bigger experiments for Singapore. If that is right, Japan's own rotation question (Brazil versus Paraguay) has a mirror on the other bench.

For the U21s, the tactical brief from Oiwa on 6 Nov still stands, because there is still no camp report: can this side show its idea against a strong home team, and can it find space and change tempo after creating chances and not taking them in the Asian Games final? Spain will arrive late on Wednesday and only train at the stadium on Thursday. They are also a midfielder down if Chema Andrés remains unreplaced. That is a small, concrete fact, not a predicted XI.

**Sweep 3b.** There is now a camp report, but it does not change the football question. JFA's own post says the rain session was used to check the team concept, with lots of instruction from Oiwa. That is consistent with the 6 Nov brief, and it still is not a team sheet. Spain have arrived, Chema is still unreplaced, and Gordo has not yet spoken. The predicted-XI picture is unchanged: only the pre-camp 4-2-3-1 and 4-3-3 already on file. They agree on Pisano, the back four of Umeki, Ichihara, Kita and Kosugi, and Shiogai. They do not agree on the midfield or the right side.

On the senior side, the striker argument is now sharper than "Maeda or Ogawa". Four dated full XIs plus Motokawa's lean leave three first-choice answers: Maeda, Ogawa and Goto. Yuito appearing as the left shadow or inside attacker in three separate back-three graphics (Ekuni, Sakanowa, soccer-kickoff) is the other detail worth using. The live preview still has Kamada in that space and Tanaka next to Sano. That is a real disagreement, not a rounding error. Watanabe versus Seko on the right of a back three is the third split. None of this should be flattened into a single "most likely" graphic.
